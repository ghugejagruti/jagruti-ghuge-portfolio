02The question

Where does community happen when traditional third places fade?

Parks, markets, and neighbourhood spaces have traditionally offered places to connect beyond home and work.

As work, responsibilities, and digital habits change, we wanted to understand what people turn to instead.

Our question

What makes an alternative community space meaningful, accessible, and worth returning to?

    Home
  2. Second place

  3. Third places

06What we found

Four patterns helped us understand changing community needs.

01
Time

Time changes how we socialise

Work, responsibilities, and planning fatigue make spontaneous participation harder.

02
Shared interests

Shared interests create meaningful connection

People seek communities built around hobbies, passions, learning, and a sense of purpose.

03
Belonging

Social chemistry matters more than social quantity

Comfort, familiarity, and shared values can matter more than simply being around more people.

04
Physical + digital

Digital convenience doesn't replace physical connection

Digital and hybrid spaces offer flexibility, while physical interactions continue to provide presence, spontaneity, and emotional depth.

NoteThese four clusters synthesise the team's broader findings into a concise portfolio narrative. The full research identified eight themes.

08Things I learned

What research taught me about community.

01

Interviews are only the beginning.

The value of qualitative research comes from finding patterns across conversations, not treating every response as an isolated opinion.

02

Coding makes ambiguity easier to work with.

Turning responses into codes helped me organise observations and build a clearer path toward themes.

03

Research becomes stronger through synthesis.

My initial themes became part of a larger team analysis, showing me how individual perspectives can contribute to a more complete understanding.

04

Findings matter when they inform decisions.

Research can move beyond describing people's experiences to identifying opportunities for more meaningful, accessible community design.

Final reflection
Community is not defined by a place alone. It's shaped by the conditions that make people feel connected, comfortable, and willing to return.
