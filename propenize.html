01

Objective

Tokenization and KYC compliance are concepts almost nobody walks in already understanding. Ask someone to put real money into something they can't explain, and they won't no matter how sound the underlying product is. The design problem wasn't the transaction. It was trust, built for a category with no existing mental model.

The Research

MENTAL MODEL MAPPING COMPARATIVE PLATFORM ANALYSIS FLOW ANALYSIS DOMAIN RESEARCH (KYC, TOKENIZATION, FRACTIONAL OWNERSHIP)
01

Blockchain has no mental model. Stock trading does.

Nobody needed to understand blockchain. They needed the platform to feel like something they already trusted. Every flow I designed borrows its structure from Zerodha and Upstox — not because it's derivative, but because that familiarity is what gets a first-time user from 'I don't get this' to 'okay, I can do this.'

02

KYC friction was cognitive, not technical

Users understood what information was being asked for. What they didn't understand was why. 'Why does a property investment app need my PAN?' — removing the 'why do you need this' friction at every step, by sequencing and wording it the way Indian users had already seen it in Groww and Zerodha, was the fix.

03

Checkout had one screen too many

The flow was always going to redirect to a payment gateway. So token quantity and pricing on a separate pre-gateway screen was pure overhead — it added a decision point without adding information. Moving those to the product page itself removed one screen between intent and action.

04

Property listings needed trust signals, not education

A first-time user didn't need to understand what tokenization meant before proceeding. They needed to see verification badges, RERA registration, a donut chart showing ownership distribution, and contextual tooltips that answered 'is this safe?' without requiring prior knowledge.

04

The Solution

Four interconnected surfaces, each with a specific trust job to do. The dashboard reads like a brokerage app not a crypto wallet. The KYC flow sequences and words each step the way an Indian user has already seen it in Groww. The checkout got shorter, not longer. The property listing leads with verification before description. Together, they move a user from unfamiliar category to committed investor without ever asking them to understand blockchain first.

05
HIGHLIGHT OF THE PROJECT

"98% of users who started KYC, completed it."

Out of 200 total signups, 80 went on to become paying investors — both first-time retail buyers and HNIs. For a category most people walk in not understanding, that completion rate is the number that matters most. It means the trust-first approach in the KYC flow worked — not just in theory, but for the actual first users who went through it.

07

What I Learned

01

Designing for an unfamiliar domain is about borrowing trust, not building it from scratch

The insight that unblocked everything: nobody needed to understand blockchain. They needed the platform to feel like something they already trusted. Finding that existing mental model — stock trading — and building on top of it was the single most important design decision on the project.

02

Research the constraint first, design the screen second

Tokenization, KYC, fractional ownership — none of that was vocabulary I walked in with. I had to learn what these terms actually meant before I could decide how they should look on screen. That changed the order I worked in: understand the domain constraint first, design the interaction second. Never the other way round.
