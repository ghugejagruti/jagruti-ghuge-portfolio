Objective

Evaluate LittleBox's existing site using a full usability method set — not just instinct — and then redesign the landing page so every change traces directly back to a specific finding. No hunches. Every decision accountable to data.

02

The Research

HEURISTIC EVALUATION SUS (SYSTEM USABILITY SCALE) NASA TLX COGNITIVE WALKTHROUGH HICK'S LAW TIMING FITTS' LAW TIMING
01

SUS score of 36.79 — structurally broken, not surface-level

Average SUS of 36.79 (range 12.5–62). No user rated it above "marginal". That range isn't noise — it reflects consistent structural failure across different users, not individual preference.

02

Experts spiked too — NASA TLX confirmed friction is built in

Both novice and expert users scored high on cognitive load. When experts struggle as much as novices, the problem isn't onboarding. It's the site's architecture.

03

Hick's Law: Task 2 peaked at 1.89s decision time

Too many ungrouped choices dumped into one navigation dropdown. Every additional option added to the decision time. Users weren't slower — the interface made them slower.

04

Fitts' Law: Task 4 jumped to 4.2s (others under 1.1s)

One specific CTA was either too small or positioned too far from the user's natural scan path. The 4× time spike on a single task pointed directly to a repositioning fix.

The Solution

Every change in the redesign traced back to a specific finding. The heuristic evaluation and eye-tracking data told us exactly where the load was concentrated — not guesses, not preferences. The team split by users (4 users per person), ran the full method set independently, then regrouped to identify shared patterns. I owned my 4 users end-to-end and then redesigned the landing page solo. The principle throughout: the redesign added nothing new. It removed everything that was in the way.

WHAT IT DOES
✦

Hick's Law → nav restructured: fewer simultaneous tabs, items revealed progressively on hover

✦

Fitts' Law → CTAs enlarged, repositioned into primary scan path

✦

Recognition over recall → 'Raise a Ticket' relabelled to 'Cancel Order'

✦

Eye-tracking data → color + delivery-date filters added exactly where users abandoned the filter panel

✦

Visual hierarchy corrected → key CTAs no longer blend into the background

✦

Cognitive walkthrough findings → task flows reduced to minimum necessary steps

05
HIGHLIGHT OF THE PROJECT

"The redesign added nothing new. It removed everything that was in the way."

Navigation got lighter — fewer tabs, but each one earned its place. Visuals replaced long text in dropdowns so recognition happened before reading did. Filters became visible instead of buried. A SUS score of 36.79 doesn't just say 'this is bad.' It tells you the problem is structural — and structural problems tell you exactly where to look. That specificity is what makes evaluation frameworks worth using.

07

What I Learned

01

Evaluation frameworks are not academic exercises

A SUS score of 36.79 doesn't just say 'this is bad.' It tells you the problem is structural and structural problems tell you exactly where to look. The data collapses ambiguity. That's what makes it worth the effort.

02

Accessibility is a design decision, not a checklist

Building for a wide range of people meant simplifying flows and clarifying content before adding anything new. The most accessible version of LittleBox wasn't a version with more features — it was one with fewer obstacles.

03

Interaction design is behaviour design

Feedback, affordances, and clear navigation didn't just look better — they changed what users were confident enough to try. Good interaction design expands the set of actions a person believes is available to them.
