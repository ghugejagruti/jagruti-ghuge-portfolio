01Who It's For

Different gardeners. One shared need: healthier plants with less effort.

Audience 01

Home gardeners

Want healthier plants, less frequent watering, and a simpler care routine.

Audience 02

Nursery owners

Need practical ways to maintain plant moisture during handling, shipping, and customer handover.

Audience 03 · Based in USA

Gift shop owners

Want a product that retains moisture to improve plant survival during transit, and need eco-friendly alternatives to synthetic moisture solutions.

The challenge was to communicate Plant Diaper's value to people with different gardening needs without assuming they already understood the product.

03What the Research Revealed

The product needed explanation before it could earn trust.

My research and competitor analysis highlighted four opportunities.

01

Explain the mechanism

Show what Plant Diaper does and how it works, rather than relying on product claims alone.

02

Make the benefit tangible

Connect water retention to everyday gardening needs and understandable outcomes.

03

Build confidence before purchase

Use clear information, visual demonstrations, and supporting evidence to help customers assess the product.

04

Support different buying contexts

Address the needs of individual gardeners as well as nursery owners and gift shop owners.

The opportunity was to make the website more than a product catalogue: it needed to help people understand the product before asking them to buy it.

04From Research to Experience

The website became a sequence of understanding, confidence, and action.

Instead of presenting information as disconnected sections, I organised the experience around the questions a potential customer might ask.

  1. Stage 1

    DISCOVER

    Introduce the gardening problem and Plant Diaper's value proposition.

  2. Stage 2

    UNDERSTAND

    Explain the product, its mechanism, and how it fits into a gardening routine.

  3. Stage 3

    EVALUATE

    Present supporting information, benefits, and relevant comparisons.

  4. Stage 4

    ACT

    Give users a clear path to explore products, calculate potential savings, or make a purchase.

07Things I Learned
01

UX begins before the interface.

When a product's positioning, sizing, or offering is still being defined, design needs to consider those upstream business decisions. The experience cannot be separated from what the business promises.

02

Information order shapes persuasion.

A website is not just a collection of screens. Introducing the problem, explaining the product, establishing credibility, and then presenting a call to action creates a more considered journey.

03

Visual design shapes expectations.

Colour, spacing, imagery, and interaction cues influence how people perceive a product before they have fully understood it. Design must make the product feel approachable without relying on aesthetics alone to establish trust.

04

Claims need context.

A benefit becomes more meaningful when people understand how it works, what supports it, and how it relates to their own needs. Product education is part of the experience, not an afterthought.

Final takeaway↑ Back to top
Good product experiences don't simply ask people to buy. They help people understand why the product matters.

Plant Diaper challenged me to think beyond individual screens and consider how product positioning, research, content, and interaction work together to support a purchase decision.
